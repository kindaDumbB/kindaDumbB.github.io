Parental Controls Don't Work

Introduction

Have you ever thought your kid has been on their screen too much, or do you think that your child has developed an addiction to their phone or another screen? It's easy to think that your kid is on their screen too much, but it is not a valid comparison to when you were a kid.

Nowadays people use their phones to communicate, and removing that can have devastating effects on your child. Removing their phone should always be a last resort and always for a short period of time.

The issue with parental controls is not the controls themselves. It's the overuse or abuse of them to gain control of your child. You have to adapt your controls to what your child is and is not telling you.

Expecting them to tell you everything can be a massive oversight and can lead to mental harm, and in a few major cases, suicide.

The limits for 6-year-olds should not be applied to a 14-year-old.

The Controversial Parts

Kids will self-regulate given the chance after roughly the age of 12 and don't need your help regulating their behavior and responsibilities unless they begin seriously harming themselves.

You have to let them fall sometimes and recover for themselves. Otherwise it can feel like you are just there to control them, and that you are simply the warden of another jail they have to sit through.

School is a full-time commitment that requires many of the same skills as a job, including responsibility, consistency, time management, and enough patience to deal with that one teacher.

Studies also show that teenagers naturally have different sleep schedules than children or adults. Those schedules can be negatively affected by bedtimes that don't account for adolescent sleep patterns.

A Reminder

It's a hard balance to maintain not spoiling your kid while also not misusing/overusing parental controls. The purpose of this is to spread awareness that parental controls are not a fix-all solution.
